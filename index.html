WHY IT MATTERS

The Atkinson cycle runs through four idealized strokes — isentropic compression, constant-volume heat addition, isentropic expansion, and exhaust. Its defining trait is an expansion stroke longer than the compression stroke, extracting more work from each combustion event than a symmetric cycle would. Compare that to the Otto cycle, where compression and expansion ratios are equal: Atkinson trades peak power for thermal efficiency, which is why it shows up in hybrid engines built to sip fuel rather than sprint.

This tool draws an animated pressure–volume diagram and a piston-and-crank schematic of the Atkinson cycle, redrawn live as you move the compression and expansion ratio sliders.
